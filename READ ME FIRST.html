What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC for everything, including the CalculiX engineering check. (On a Mac or Linux computer, everything except the CalculiX check works: see Mac & Linux.)
  • A web browser: Chrome, Edge or Firefox. Cyclic Labs runs in your browser but entirely on your computer: no internet, no account, nothing uploaded.
  • About 25 MB of disk space. No administrator rights. Nothing gets installed into Windows.

Install in 3 steps

  1. Download the zip Download the latest Cyclic-Labs zip from the project's Releases page on GitHub (or Code → Download ZIP) to your computer (for example your Downloads folder).
  2. Extract it (important) Right-click the zip → Extract All… → pick where to keep it (Documents is a good place) → Extract. You get a folder called Cyclic Labs.
    Don't run anything from inside the zip (double-clicking into a zip only shows a preview). If you do, Cyclic Labs tells you it can't find its files: extract first.
  3. Start it Open the Cyclic Labs folder and double-click Start Cyclic Labs.
    • A small window titled Cyclic Labs opens. Leave it open while you use Cyclic Labs: it's the local helper that runs the CalculiX solver. Close it when you're done.
    • Cyclic Labs opens in your browser at http://localhost:8765. (localhost means "this computer": nothing goes online.)
    • The first time, a short walkthrough helps you set up your first bike.

The first time only: Windows may ask if you trust it

Files downloaded from the internet get a safety check. You may see one of these. Both are normal for small free programs that aren't from a big software company:

  • "Windows protected your PC" (blue box): click More info, then Run anyway.
  • "Open File – Security Warning: The publisher could not be verified": click Run. (Untick "Always ask before opening this file" to stop it asking.)

On its first start, the helper also clears that "downloaded" mark from Cyclic Labs' own files, so the solver can start. It doesn't touch anything else on your computer.

Tip: put it on your desktop. Right-click Start Cyclic Labs → Show more options (Windows 11) → Send to → Desktop (create shortcut).

Mac & Linux

Extract the zip and double-click Open on Mac or Linux.html (or app/index.html). Everything works (design, 3D, overlay, parts, drawing scanner, checks, stress, fatigue and lab-test estimates) except the CalculiX check card, which needs the Windows helper.

The CalculiX solver (the engineering software)

CalculiX is a free, professional finite-element (FEA) solver: the kind of engineering software used to check parts for stress. Cyclic Labs uses it in Design + Engineering mode to double-check its own quick frame model and to build detailed 3D models of the weld joints.

It's optional, and not included. Everything except the CalculiX check and 3D weld models works without it. To add it (Windows): download calculix_2.23_4win.zip from www.dhondt.de (the CalculiX author's site), right-click it → Extract All… into the system\solver folder, then start Cyclic Labs again. "Start Cyclic Labs" finds it automatically.

Check that it's working

  1. Start Cyclic Labs with Start Cyclic Labs. The helper window should say CalculiX solver: …\system\solver\…\ccx_2.23.exe.
  2. In Cyclic Labs, click Design + Engineering (top right) and scroll to the CalculiX check card. It should name the solver it found.
  3. Click Run CalculiX check. After a few seconds a table appears comparing CalculiX with Cyclic Labs' quick model. If you get the table, CalculiX is working.

"3D weld models (every joint)" in the same card builds a solid model of each weld and takes a few minutes per joint. Leave the page open while it runs.

If the card says the solver isn't available

The card says…What to do
"To use CalculiX, start Cyclic Labs with Start Cyclic Labs…"You opened the page directly (for example from app\index.html). Close it and use Start Cyclic Labs.
"The Cyclic Labs helper window isn't running"The black/blue Cyclic Labs window was closed. Double-click Start Cyclic Labs again.
"CalculiX solver not found"CalculiX isn't in system\solver yet (see above), or antivirus quarantined it. Extract the CalculiX zip there again.

Already have CalculiX installed somewhere else? Make a text file called ccx_path.txt next to Start Cyclic Labs containing one line, the full path to the ccx program, for example:

C:\CalculiX\ccx_2.23.exe

Your first 10 minutes

  1. Walkthrough. The first time, pick road / gravel / mountain, the wheel size, then either a typical size (S–XL), your own numbers, a brand's geometry chart (copy and paste it from their website), a BikeCAD file, or a scan of a builder's drawing. Add your weight and shoe size and Cyclic Labs builds a starting bike. You can re-run it any time with + New bike.
  2. Look around. There's also a ready-made Example – Ti 29er singlespeed hardtail in the Design list. Drag the 3D bike to turn it; scroll to zoom.
  3. Change a number. Everything on the left (reach, stack, head angle, chainstay, tube sizes…) updates the bike and every check as you type. Undo edits puts it back.
  4. Or just type it. Click Design chat and write things like make the reach 460, use a 140 mm fork, head angle 1° slacker, shoe size 11 US, what's the trail?, undo.
  5. Save. Save as new keeps a copy so you can compare versions (Compare with, top right of the page). Export file saves it as a file you can keep, email, or send to your builder.
  6. Take the tour. Tutorial (top right) walks through every panel, and has a step-by-step "refine my bike" guide.

What it can do

Geometry & fitReach, stack, angles, trail, wheelbase, BB height, effective top tube, mixed wheel sizes (mullet), 26" to 32" wheels.
Builder checklistTyre and chainring clearance, pedal clearance, seatpost fit and insertion, dropper fit, tube sizes and small parts, each with the rule it comes from.
Toe overlapYour shoe size (EU/US/UK, up to XXXL feet), shoe type, cleat position and mudguards: how far you can turn the bars before your toe touches the tyre.
Scan a drawingRead a builder's frame drawing (PDF, picture or phone photo) into a new design, then check the numbers on a review screen.
ImportsBikeCAD (.bcad) files and geometry charts pasted from any brand's website.
Tubes & staysDiameter and wall of every tube, butted ends, standard Ti tube shapes, bent or wishbone seat stays, chainstay yoke, bridge.

The Strength (FEA) tab

In step 2 (Design), the column beside the bike has two tabs: Design and Strength (FEA). Open the Strength tab and click Turn on the strength simulation to add the stress side (also under Help). You'll be asked to read and accept a short disclaimer first. It runs in the background either way; this just shows it.

Simulation only. It may contain mistakes. The strength results are rough estimates from a simplified model, not an engineering approval or a safety certification. Always have your frame builder or a qualified frame designer review and approve the design. See DISCLAIMER.txt.
These are estimates, for comparing designs. Your builder has the final say: they run their own analysis and know their frames, so a red or amber number here is a question to ask them, not a reason to drop a design. Want more margin anyway? Ask for bigger or thicker tubing; builders will usually do it.
  • Stress colours & animations: the frame coloured by how hard each part works for each riding load (sprints, braking, rock hits, huck to flat, landing on the front), with flex shown.
  • Riding you set: terrain (road to bike park), riding style, sprint power, hours per week, biggest drop, fork travel.
  • Fatigue life & abuse: a rough estimate of weld fatigue life for that riding, and what wears them most. Best for comparing designs.
  • Comfort explorer: try one change at a time, or let the optimiser find the plushest frame that still meets the rules you set.
  • CalculiX check and 3D weld models: see above.

Where your bikes are saved (read this once)

Cyclic Labs keeps your saved designs and parts inside your web browser on this computer, not in the Cyclic Labs folder. That means:

  • Always open it the same way (with Start Cyclic Labs) in the same browser. Designs saved in Chrome don't show in Edge, and designs saved with "Start Cyclic Labs" don't show in the "(no CalculiX)" version.
  • Back up with Export file. It saves the open design as a .json file. Import file brings it back on any computer or browser, and that's also how you share a design with a friend or your builder.
  • Clearing your browser's history / site data for localhost deletes saved designs. Export the ones you care about first.
  • Parts you add yourself: Build my bike → My parts library → Export my parts saves them to a file too.

Updating & removing

Installing a newer version

  1. Close the Cyclic Labs helper window.
  2. (Optional but wise) Export the designs you care about first.
  3. Extract the new zip (it makes a new folder, e.g. Cyclic Labs 9.3) and start it with its Start Cyclic Labs. Your saved designs are still there, because they live in the browser at the same address.
  4. Delete the old folder once you're happy (and update your desktop shortcut).

Removing Cyclic Labs

Close the helper window and delete the folder. That's all: nothing was installed into Windows. (Saved designs stay in your browser's storage for localhost:8765 until you clear that site's data.)

What the numbers mean (and don't)

Cyclic Labs models each tube as a titanium beam with the sizes you enter, welded at the joints, with weld-zone strength and a welded-joint fatigue curve. It is very good at comparing designs: "is B stiffer, lighter, longer-lasting than A, and by how much?"

It is not an engineering certification. Stress, fatigue-life and lab-test results are estimates from a simplified model. Fatigue years in particular depend on assumptions about how often big hits happen: use them to compare designs, not as a promise. The software may also contain bugs, and numbers typed in or scanned can be wrong. A pass here doesn't mean a frame is safe and a fail doesn't mean it's unsafe. Your frame builder is responsible for the final design, materials, welding and testing. Always have your builder or a qualified frame designer review the design, and confirm anything flagged against your builder's drawing. Don't use Cyclic Labs to decide whether a frame is safe to ride. Full text: DISCLAIMER.txt.

The How to read the results box on the Strength (FEA) tab explains the method, and every checklist line says where its rule comes from.
